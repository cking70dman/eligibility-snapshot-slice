Literature Review

Seven evidence-based findings that frame the IS-001 design agenda.

Finding 01

Digital access is high but uneven

97%+ of veterans use the internet, but older and rural veterans show lower digital literacy. Design must not assume fluency — onboarding language, help text, and error recovery must all be written for a range of comfort levels.

Finding 02

Trust is the primary conversion barrier

Veterans distrust government digital systems. Trust signals — plain language, visible human fallback options, no-PII data handling — are the primary UX levers for converting initial engagement into task completion.

Finding 03

Cognitive load from benefits complexity is the core UX problem

The fragmented program landscape produces high cognitive load at every decision point. A navigator UI must reduce perceived complexity without making legal or financial determinations on behalf of the user.

Finding 04

Form-based interfaces consistently underperform

Conversational and wizard-style flows with contextual help show better task completion rates for this population compared to traditional form interfaces. Progressive disclosure reduces abandonment.

Finding 05

Mobile-first is mandatory

Younger post-9/11 veterans skew mobile-primary. Rural veterans frequently use mobile as their only internet-connected device. Desktop-first design systematically excludes key segments of the target population.

Finding 06

Accessibility gaps are structural

VA.gov and most federal tools fail WCAG 2.1 AA in practice — particularly for cognitive accessibility, TBI, and PTSD-affected users. Structural accessibility must be a design constraint from the first sketch, not a retrofit.

Finding 07

Co-design with veterans improves adoption

Participatory design methods produce measurably higher task success rates than tools designed without veteran input. IS-001 prioritizes veteran recruitment for usability testing in both rounds.

Strategic Implications for Chief Bud

Seven design and product decisions supported by the competitive landscape.

  • No existing tool combines veteran-specific targeting, no-PII intake, and conversational flow. Chief Bud occupies a genuine white space.
  • The dominant input pattern (form/search) is the weakest performer for veteran task completion — Chief Bud's wizard-style snapshot is competitively differentiated.
  • Federal tools earn trust through brand authority but lose it through usability. Chief Bud must earn trust through interaction quality and transparent data handling.
  • Mobile-first must be an engineering constraint, not a responsive afterthought. At least 40% of veteran digital interactions happen on mobile.
  • Output format matters as much as input format — referral lists without pathway context produce abandonment. Chief Bud must frame outputs as next steps, not lists.
  • DAV Navigator is the closest comparable in terms of veteran-centric intake, but lacks housing depth. Chief Bud should monitor DAV Navigator roadmap for convergence risk.
  • Accessibility is a market differentiator, not just a compliance requirement. Most competitors fail WCAG 2.1 AA for cognitive accessibility — Chief Bud can lead on this dimension.
